for Kopimap

Proposal only. No application code changed. Nothing under client/, server/, config, the database or production was touched, and nothing was deployed.

This report is docs/redesign/nota-neutral/index.html. Its assets sit beside it in docs/redesign/nota-neutral/: map.svg, drawn from OpenStreetMap roads of Solo, and shop photos from the live API (p-*.jpg, t-*.jpg). Shop names, hours, ratings and coordinates come from https://kopimap.mwyndham.dev/api/coffees, read on 29 October 2026 (150 shops). Distances are measured from a visitor on Jl. Slamet Riyadi near Sriwedari.

The first pass, docs/redesign/opus/index.html, is unchanged; this pass changes colour and texture only.

Kopimap becomes the back counter of a kopi bar in Solo. The list is a receipt that prints out of a till. Every shop you open is a ticket clipped to a steel rail and tied to its spot on the map with a thread. Editing is writing on a pink carbon nota. Everything is paper, ink and steel, and every edge is cut square or torn.

Changes in this pass

  1. No more sepia. Every grey is now a true neutral, with equal red, green and blue: the counter, the map, the steel, the till and the newsprint. The first pass tinted all of them warm, which read as old rather than physical.
  2. Colour moved onto objects. Tickets are mint index card, the edit nota is pink carbonless, the selection is fluorescent lime, the thread and rating cups are signal orange, Save is an orange anodised key and Add is a cobalt one. Each colour still has one job.
  3. More hardware texture. The till is perforated graphite, the rail and keys have a specular streak and a concave cap, the VFD has a glass reflection, and the page sits on terrazzo. The tickets get a perforated tear line.
  4. The map is neutral. White roads on grey land, blue water and green parks. The ochre is gone, so the orange thread and the lime selection are the loudest things on it.
  5. Error lines read on pink. Red text on the pink nota fell to 3.6:1, so errors are now white on a red label.
  6. Unchanged: the layout, the rail, the thread, the till, the halftone thumbnails, the type and every interaction.

1. What the current UI is, read from the code

The redesign in docs/redesign.md fixed the structure: one panel, edit in place, honest empty states. What it kept is the look of every other map app. These are the specific reasons, with the lines that cause them.

  1. It is the stock map-app shell. Desktop is a 400px card with lg:rounded-box lg:shadow-xl floating on the left (client/src/routes/-panel.tsx:64). Phones get a rounded-t-[1.25rem] bottom sheet with a 40×4px pill handle (-panel.tsx:87) and a round floating btn-circle btn-lg “+” (-panel.tsx:69). That is the Google Maps and Material kit, part for part. Nothing on screen is about coffee or about Solo.
  2. The surface has no material. The light theme is three almost identical greys with no chroma: base-100 98%, base-200 97%, base-300 92% lightness, with daisyUI’s --depth: 0 and --noise: 0 (client/src/index.css:19-21, 45-46). The only identity left is one green and one blue.
  3. “Reviewed” changes colour at night. --color-primary is green in light mode (index.css:23, oklch 45% 0.15 150) and coral red in dark mode (index.css:59, oklch 66% 0.2 22). The pin, chip and list star that mean “reviewed” change hue with the OS theme.
  4. Status is colour on identical shapes. Known and reviewed pins use the same PIN_PATH and differ only by stroke colour and stroke width, 1.7 against 2.6 (-mapLayers/-coffeeMarkers.tsx:52-54). The selected pin is the same teardrop, filled, with an animate-ping halo (-mapLayers/-coffeeMarker.tsx:58-61), which is the “blue dot” idiom. In the list, a reviewed ★ 3.5 and a rated-but-not-reviewed ★ 3.5 differ only by text-primary (-contentTab/-common/-cofeeListItem.tsx:58-61), and 20 known shops carry a rating.
  5. The exception is printed as the rule. 126 of 150 rows print “Not reviewed” in accent blue (-cofeeListItem.tsx:62-63), and only 3 shops are reviewed. The word that repeats on almost every row is the one that should stand out least.
  6. District labels leak street names and plus codes. area() (-util.ts:19-20) looks for “Kec.” and otherwise takes the first comma part. 29 of 150 addresses are in Google’s English format, so rows show “Slamet Riyadi St No.241”, “CRJ8+9QG”, “Parking area” or “Dusun III” where the district belongs, although those addresses do name it (“…, Sriwedari, Laweyan, Surakarta City”).
  7. The panel’s height ignores its content. The default tab is Latest reviews (client/src/store/store.ts:69), which has 3 shops, but the panel always runs from top-4 to bottom-4. On production at 1440×900 the three rows fill about 180px of an 868px card. The rest is empty grey.
  8. One shop at a time, no trail. Browse and Shop swap inside the same column (-panel.tsx:110-116). Comparing three shops means Back, scroll, open, Back. J and K only walk the list a shop was opened from (-shortcuts.tsx:56-61), and nothing remembers what you looked at a minute ago.
  9. Rounded everything. rounded-box (0.75rem) appears 12 times, rounded-full 7 times and rounded-field 3 times, on top of daisyUI’s own radii on every btn, input, badge, tab, kbd and modal-box.
  10. The sparse-shop machinery now rarely shows. redesign.md was written when 50 shops had no address. Today the API returns 0 shops without an address, 1 without hours (Arzi Coffee), and a thumbnail for every shop. The real gaps are reviews (3 of 150) and ratings (23 of 150), so that is where the design should spend its attention.

The uncommon patterns

  1. The rail: history you can see and touch. Every shop you open is a ticket clipped to a steel rail along the top of the screen. The open one hangs full length. The ones you opened before ride the rail as short stubs, newest nearest. Click a stub to hang it again, press × to take it down, and the oldest falls off the end when the rail is full (three stubs at 1440px, two at 1280px, one at 1024px, two on a phone). It replaces Back with something you can see, and it doubles as a compare tray: the hours and status of the last three shops stay on screen. [ and ] walk the rail; J and K still walk the list.
  2. The tether: a thread instead of a forced centre. The open ticket has a square eyelet, and a thread runs from it to its stamp on the map. The thread sags like string. When you pan, it follows; when the shop leaves the screen, it runs to the edge and keeps pointing. Nobody has to hunt for “which pin is this”, and the map no longer has to fly the shop into a gap beside the panel to make the link obvious.
  3. The till at the thumb. On phones the sheet is paper fed up out of a till that sits on the bottom edge. Search lives in the till’s display, where the thumb already is. The till’s keys change with the paper on top of it: search on the receipt, Back, Directions, Copy and Read review on a ticket, Cancel and Save on a nota. The main action of every view is always in the same place, at the bottom.
  4. The paper tells you the mode. White thermal paper is browsing, mint card is a shop, pink carbon is editing. You can tell edit mode from across the room, without reading a word.

Saving and uploading: two waits, one bar

A save is two different waits. The shop row is one request whose length the browser cannot see. The photos are streams whose bytes it can count. Both are reported on the same object: a strip of thermal tape in a slot cut into the nota footer, with the print head sitting at the end of what has printed. The unknowable wait gets a head that sweeps; the countable one gets tape that advances under it.

Save and upload

PHOTO 2 OF 345%
  1. One slot, both waits, no jump. The row is a printed receipt line (label, dot leader, value) over a 14px slot in the nota footer: directly above Save on desktop, directly above the till on a phone. Its height is always reserved, and the slot is empty tape when idle, so starting a save never moves the form.
  2. Saving the shop row is a carriage return. The client cannot know how long the server takes, so no percentage is invented. The head crosses the tape in fifteen steps at 90ms, dragging a 30% smear of halftone that fades behind it; at the right edge it snaps back and crosses again, the way a print head returns for the next line. The label reads SAVING, the right side counts elapsed seconds, and the tape’s 5% notches stay empty.
  3. Photos advance the tape. Each file reports upload.onprogress, weighted by its size. The head parks at the edge of what has printed and the halftone advances one 5% notch at a time. The left label names the photo (PHOTO 2 OF 3, or just PHOTO when there is one) and the right shows the percentage. Bytes fill the tape only to 95%; the last notch prints when the server answers, so a slow server after the bytes are sent holds at 95% with the seconds counting instead of sitting at a false 100%. If a browser cannot report progress, the tape steps once per finished file, which is the estimate, and the label carries the count instead.
  4. Stalls, closes and double taps. If no bytes move for 10s the right side switches from the percentage to elapsed seconds; nothing else changes until the request errors or times out (60s), which tears the tape. Closing the tab or the nota while printing asks first (beforeunload, and the existing discard confirm). Save is aria-disabled while printing and ignores taps, so a double submit cannot post twice. Empty or unsupported files are refused when picked, on the contact sheet, never at Save.
  5. The end has a beat. The head parks at the right, the tape is fully printed and reads PRINTED for 300ms, then the existing drain runs: the nota lifts and turns mint. Without the pause the drain starts before the eye has read the result.
  6. Failure tears the tape. The shop row is saved before the photos, so a failed upload must not lose it. The head stops, the tape gets a 2px red dashed tear at the jam point, the label reads PHOTO 2 FAILED and the right side 1 SENT, and the existing error label appears under the tear line. Save returns as Retry 2 photos and sends only the photos not yet confirmed (the failed one and those queued after it), against the saved shop id; on edit, the row is re-sent only if a field changed since. The shop stays saved: closing now keeps the shop without those photos. The bar work includes fixing today’s retry: it re-posts the shop row and re-uploads every file, which duplicates a newly added shop.
  7. No photos means no advancing tape. The head sweeps once, parks at the right, the tape prints, then the drain.
  8. Phones and Reduce Motion do not sweep. The head parks in the middle of the tape and only the seconds move; the smear is hidden. The measured advance still moves, because it reports data rather than decoration, in 5% steps with no transition. The Save key on the till stays pressed and reads the phase.
  9. Announced once per change. The track is role="progressbar" with aria-valuenow only while determinate, and no value while indeterminate; its aria-label follows the phase (Saving shop, Uploading photos). Only the left label is aria-live="polite", so it speaks on phase and photo, never on a percent or a second. The form carries aria-busy="true"; Cancel is disabled, and Save stays pressed at full contrast with aria-disabled so the phase stays readable.

Cost and reach. The visual is one slot, one halftone band, one carriage: about thirty lines of CSS, no images. The real work is the upload helper: fetch only reports completion, so the photo step moves to XMLHttpRequest for upload.onprogress. The save keeps a phase (idle, saving, printing), the saved shop id, and the queue of photos still to send, so Retry resumes instead of starting over. About half a day, mostly the retry path.

    Shape rules

    1. No radius, anywhere. One rule, *, ::before, ::after { border-radius: 0 }, plus overrides for MapLibre’s attribution and control CSS.
    2. No circles either. Pins, clusters, bolts, LEDs, eyelets and the you-are-here mark are squares, hexagons or diamonds. Curves only appear in type, photos and map geometry.
    3. Four edges. Straight cut for card. Torn, 14px teeth, for thermal paper only. Perforated, 4px dashes, between a stub and its tear line and under the receipt’s count. Double frame for stamps.
    4. Rotation is for ink. Stamps and chops turn between −4° and +4°, seeded from the shop id so a shop always looks the same. Paper hangs straight. The one exception is the clipping, at −0.6°.
    5. Border weights. 1px ink at 25–35% for paper edges and dividers. 1.5px ink for fields, boxes and tokens. 2px ink for focus, crop marks and the keyboard highlight. 3px only for the focused field’s rule.
    6. Two shadows. Paper: a 1px contact line and an 18px soft drop. Key: a 2px hard bottom and a 5px soft drop. Light comes from the top-left. Pressing a key removes its hard bottom and moves it down 2px.
    7. Targets. Keys are 40px on desktop and 44–46px on phones. Receipt rows are 58px. Fact rows are 36px on desktop and 44px on phones.

    13. Risks

    1. Kitsch. Skeuomorphism turns into a theme park fast. The guard is one source of objects (the counter), materials instead of fake 3D, no wood, leather or perspective, and grain capped at 10%. Judge it on a real phone at 1×, with real shop names, before polishing anything.
    2. Legibility on texture. Grain lowers effective contrast a little. Text always sits on a flat paper colour with at least 4.5:1 before grain, and the tightest pair, chop on ticket at 4.97, is only used for bold stamps.
    3. Performance. Drop-shadow filters on torn paper, the rough-ink filter and the map grain all cost GPU time. Mitigations: static noise tiles, the rough filter on at most ~10 HTML elements, pin roughness baked into images, and no blend mode over the WebGL canvas.
    4. The thread. It has to track the map at 60fps and must never block a tap. It uses one SVG path updated in requestAnimationFrame with pointer-events: none, and when the shop is off screen it clamps to the edge with an arrowhead.
    5. Search at the bottom on phones. iOS Safari’s keyboard and viewport handling is the classic trap. The till rides visualViewport, and the sheet goes to Full when the display gets focus. This needs testing on iOS 17/18 Safari and Android Chrome.
    6. Map style coupling. Recolouring Stadia’s alidade layers by id at runtime breaks if the upstream style changes. Forking it to resource.mwyndham.dev/sono/ as its own JSON avoids that.
    7. Two kinds of next. J/K walk the list and [ ] walk the rail. That is clear on the shortcuts card, but it is one more idea to learn.
    8. One outlier. Deca Dessert and Coffee is in Banjarnegara, about 120 km west (110.70°E). Any “fit all shops” view or distance sort will stretch for it. It is a data fix, flagged here, not designed around.
    9. Upload progress needs XHR. fetch only reports completion, so the photo step moves to XMLHttpRequest for upload.onprogress. The retry path also needs the saved shop id and the unsent files kept, otherwise a second Save after a failed photo re-creates an added shop and re-uploads the photos that already landed.

    14. Decisions I need from you

    1. The rail. Keep it as visible history (3 stubs on desktop, 2 on phones)? Should it survive a reload through localStorage? My recommendation: yes to both.
    2. Search at the bottom on phones. The till at the thumb, or the display at the top of the paper? I recommend the bottom.
    3. Dark mode. The night counter (paper stays paper, the map goes dark), or light only? I recommend the night counter, built last.
    4. Marking only exceptions. Plain known shops get no mark in the list. Is that right for you as the reviewer?
    5. The map style. Fork the style JSON (recommended), or recolour at runtime?
    6. Fonts. Big Shoulders, its stencil cut for stamps, and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono next to the Atkinson Next you already use. Yes?
    7. The thread. Always on while a ticket is open (recommended), or only while the shop is off-centre?
    8. The area() fix. Ship it in step 3, or on its own today? It is a few lines and independent of the redesign.
    9. The save bar. A strip of thermal tape in a slot in the nota footer: the print head sweeps while the shop row is saved, then parks at the edge while the tape prints the photos. No spinner, and no percentage while the shop row is saved. Yes?
