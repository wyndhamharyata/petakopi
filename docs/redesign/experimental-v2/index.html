Field Atlas

Kopimap's 150 coffee shops in Solo as a printed field atlas: a recoloured map plate with a lettered grid, an index sheet, record cards and ruled forms. This is the second pass of the same direction, rebuilt on live data and checked against the app's own rules. No application code was changed.

Changes in this pass

The direction stays: one physical object, a printed field atlas, with every screen a part of it. What changed is everything the first pass got wrong when held against the code, the live data and the app's own rules in docs/redesign.md.

Kept on purpose

Brass tacks, rubber stamps, ink stars, ruled fields, the press-to-stamp checkbox, folder tabs, the recoloured style, the palette and Atkinson for body text.

Criticism I did not accept

That folder tabs are the wrong pattern. Their size was wrong; the pattern is the most legible part of the sheet.

Partly fair

That the record card is too dense. Its content is the app's own list and stays. The density came from tiny type and a clipped card; it now scrolls at full size.

The object

A field atlas is a map plate, an index and a set of record cards, printed on paper, carried to the shop and marked up by hand. Kopimap already has all three: the map, the list and the shop view. The design makes them that object instead of a floating panel over a map.

  • Paper and ink. Radius 0. Depth comes from one sheet lying on another and one cast shadow. Rules are 1px, focus is 2px red. The only curves are drawn marks: tack heads, stars, the north arrow.
  • The map is a plate. It has a neatline and margins. The margins carry the grid letters and numbers, the scale bar and north. The sheet lies on the plate, not in front of a background.
  • Marks are annotation. Brass tack: known. Red tack: reviewed. Dark crossed tack: closed. Each view has one filled red control.
  • One sheet at a time. Browse is the index, a shop is its record card, and editing is the same card as a ruled form.
  • Not costume. No coffee stains, torn edges, sepia text, paper sounds or page curls. The material decides shape, hierarchy and controls, then stops.

The plate and the index

Hover a row and its tack is marked on the plate with registration ticks while its grid letter and number light up in the margins. Hover a tack and its row lights up. The search field filters all 150 shops, the tabs switch between the 3 reviews and the full index, ↑ ↓ Enter work in the search field, and the Keys button or ? opens the key legend.

The plate

The recolour stays as the first pass proposed it: fetch alidade_smooth_light.json, overwrite the paint of all 53 layers (29 by id, and the 24 label layers by type), then hand the object to MapLibre. There are no new tiles, no new provider and no new key.

The grain is new. It is one PNG tile added with map.addImage, one GeoJSON polygon covering the world, and one fill layer with fill-pattern, inserted under the first label layer. It is drawn in the same GPU pass as the roads, it pans with the map like printed paper, and the labels stay crisp. Under prefers-contrast: more, or if a phone drops frames, one call hides it: setLayoutProperty('paper-grain', 'visibility', 'none').

The grid

The plate carries a grid of 0.01° squares, about 1.1 km on a side. It starts at 110.74°E, 7.53°S, with columns A–K (no I, as in printed atlases) and rows 1–8. 149 of the 150 shops fall on it. Deca Dessert and Coffee, in Banjarnegara 120 km west, reads "Off plate". A reference is a pure function of latitude and longitude, so it never changes with sort or search.

  • Why a grid. Every shop gets a short, stable address you can say aloud ("Couvee's in H4"). The index can point at the plate without moving the map. The margins become the readout when you add a shop (section 04).
  • How. The lines are a GeoJSON line layer at 20% ink. The margin letters and numbers are DOM labels placed with map.project() on move. The margins cost 26px at the top and 30px on the right and bottom of the map.

Marks

  • Tacks. Brass for known, red for reviewed, dark and crossed for closed. The head is 22px; the hit area stays 40px, like today's pins. Tacks are map images drawn from the tokens, as today's pins are.
  • Tags. A cluster is a paper tag with a count. Its head turns red when one of its shops is reviewed, as today's red ring does. Hovering a tag lists its first three shops with their references, the same content as today's hover card.
  • Marking. Four red registration ticks around the head and a paper slip with the name and reference. Hovering a row draws the shop's own tack over its cluster, so a clustered shop can still be found.

The index sheet

  • Rows. A 40px print, the name in Fraunces, dot leaders to the grid reference, then the app's own meta line: status or rating, area, distance. Rows are 58px, so eight fit on a 700px plate.
  • Tabs. Folder tabs with their counts, 40px tall on desktop and 44px on a phone. Reviews is still the default tab, and it holds 3 shops. While a query is typed, results come from all shops and Index shows as active. The app hides the tabs during a search; here they are the sheet's edge, so they stay.
  • Add. A red tab cut into the same edge. It is the only filled red control on the screen.
  • Footer. The tally, and a Keys button. Today the shortcut list has no visible way in.
  • Seen in the data. 126 rows say "Not reviewed", as the app does today. Arzi Coffee's area shows as its plus code, CR7F+CFQ, because the app's area() falls back to the first part of the address. That is a data fix, not a design one.

Adding a shop, and the keys

Move the pointer over the plate: a surveyor's crosshair runs to the margins, where the longitude and latitude are read off. Click to drop a draft tack and open the new-shop form. Or paste a Google Maps link into the field and press Enter.

Adding

  • The Add tab collapses the sheet to its head and the link row. The app sets the same "placing" state and shrinks the phone sheet to peek.
  • The crosshair replaces the bare crosshair cursor. Its lines end in the margins, where the coordinates are printed: the longitude in the top band, the latitude turned on its side in the right band.
  • A click drops a red draft tack with ticks and opens the form with the location filled in. The tack stays draggable while editing, as today.
  • A link with no location gets the app's own message under the rule: "That link has no shop location." A link to a saved shop opens that shop, as today.

The key legend

A printed map carries its legend in a corner of the plate, so the shortcut list opens there, not as a centred modal with a backdrop. It lists the ten entries in -shortcuts.tsx. ? or Esc closes it.

The record card

A shop is a card in the atlas: the name and grid reference in a fixed header, a print, a ledger of facts, stamped actions, the review, the comments and the nearby shops. Both cards are at full length; in the app the card body scrolls under the header.

      The order that keeps every deploy working

      • First, change values only. Set the two daisyUI themes to the atlas palette, with --radius-box, --radius-field and --radius-selector at 0. That is one file, index.css. Every component goes square and paper-coloured, and the pins repaint themselves through themeColor(). Nothing else is touched.
      • Then swap components file by file. There are 11 files, with the editor and the shop view as the bulk. Each swap is a visual check at 390px and 1440px.
      • Last, remove the plugin. Move the colour variables into a plain @theme block under the same names, so the 66 utilities and the runtime reads keep working. Delete @plugin "daisyui" when a grep for the component class names comes back empty.

      Risks, and what I need from you

      Grain on the plate

      The pattern layer adds one fill draw per frame, far less than a CSS blend, but MapLibre crossfades patterns between whole zoom levels, and that may show while pinching. Check on a mid-range Android phone in step 2. The fallback is hiding the layer and keeping the recolour.

      Margins on a phone

      The bands take 22px off the top and the right edge of a 390px screen, and the top band sits under the status bar on notched phones. It needs env(safe-area-inset-top). If it feels cramped, keep the letters and drop the right band.

      The grid's origin is permanent

      References stay fixed only while the origin does. Growing east or south adds letters or numbers. Growing west or north would renumber every shop, so the origin should be settled before launch. Today 149 of 150 shops fit.

      Covers get more visible

      A print makes a bad cover obvious. By eye, two covers are letter placeholders and roughly 25 are menus. Choosing better covers is a data task, and the form makes it one tap per shop.

      Costume creep

      Stamps are for status and Closed only, there is one filled red control per view, and Fraunces is used at 17px and up. If every label gets a stamp, the atlas turns into a theme park.

      Density

      With the 12px floor and 58px rows, a 700px desktop sheet shows 8 shops, about the same as today's 64px rows. On a phone at half height, 4 rows are visible.

      Decide

      • Thumbnails in the index. I recommend yes. All 150 shops have one, and most show the storefront or the room.
      • The grid and margins. I recommend yes. Without the margins a reference has nothing to point at, so the alternative is distance only.
      • Night desk in the same release, or later. I recommend later: step 8 is independent.
      • Step 1 as the first deploy. Theme values only, in one file. It shows the direction on the real app in a day and can be reverted in a minute.
